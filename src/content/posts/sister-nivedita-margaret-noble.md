Born Margaret Elizabeth Noble in Dungannon, County Tyrone, in 1867, the woman who would become known across India as "Sister Nivedita" ("the Dedicated One") brought the fervor of Irish Celtic heritage and Pestalozzian educational pedagogy to the heart of Bengal.

## Meeting Swami Vivekananda

In 1895, Margaret Noble met Swami Vivekananda in London. Captivated by his philosophy of Vedanta and his call for the upliftment of Indian women, she resolved to dedicate her life to India, arriving in Calcutta in January 1898.

She founded a pioneering school for girls in the conservative neighborhood of Bagbazar, going door-to-door to persuade orthodox families to educate their daughters.

## Patriotism, Science & Art

During the catastrophic Calcutta plague outbreak of 1899, Nivedita organized medical relief teams, personally nursing patients and cleaning streets. She also mentored leading pioneers of the Indian Renaissance, including physicist Sir Jagadish Chandra Bose and painter Abanindranath Tagore.

Her life stands as one of the most profound personal bridges between Ireland and India—a Northern Irish schoolteacher who became a revered national figure in the Indian freedom movement.
