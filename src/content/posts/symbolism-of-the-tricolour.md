Why do Ireland and India share the same triad of colours—saffron/orange, white, and green? While each nation imbues the colours with its unique national spirit, both tricolours were born out of 19th and 20th-century republican struggles designed to symbolize communal harmony and sovereign hope.

In Ireland, Thomas Francis Meagher unfurled the tricolour in 1848: green representing the Gaelic tradition, orange the Williamite tradition, and white the lasting truce between them. In India, Pingali Venkayya and Mahatma Gandhi designed the swaraj flag with saffron for courage and renunciation, green for prosperity and faith, and white for peace and truth.

Together, the colors embody a shared commitment to building peace across historic divides.
