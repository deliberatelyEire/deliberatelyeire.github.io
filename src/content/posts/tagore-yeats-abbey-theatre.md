In the summer of 1912, William Butler Yeats read the handwritten manuscript translations of Rabindranath Tagore's *Gitanjali* on the top of London omnibuses and in private rooms. Moved to tears, Yeats agreed to write the introduction that would introduce Tagore to the Western world and pave the way for Tagore's 1913 Nobel Prize in Literature.

Yet their connection ran far deeper than a publisher's introduction. Both men were cultural nationalists leading literary renaissances in lands striving to rediscover their native identities under foreign rule.

## The Abbey Theatre Production of The Post Office

In 1913, the Abbey Theatre in Dublin staged the world premiere of Tagore's lyrical play *The Post Office* (*Dak Ghar*), directed by Yeats and Lady Gregory to raise funds for the Dublin Municipal Gallery of Modern Art.

The Irish cast and audience felt an immediate emotional kinship with Tagore's themes of spiritual yearning, village life, and metaphysical liberation.

## An Enduring Dialogue Between Two Renaissances

Tagore and Yeats shared a conviction that political liberation without cultural awakening was hollow. While their later correspondence reflected differing views on modernism, their early collaboration remains one of the most fruitful East-West intellectual encounters of the 20th century.
