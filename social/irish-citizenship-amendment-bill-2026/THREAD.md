## Post 1/7  [229 chars]  → image: journey.png

Ireland’s draft citizenship Bill would raise the residence bar from five years to eight.

Arrive on a work permit in 2026 and a likely decision moves from about 2032 to 2035. A PhD researcher on Stamp 2 would wait about 13 years.

Alt: Chart titled One arrival, January 2026, five permissions, showing the first year each person could apply for Irish citizenship now and under the 2026 General Scheme, plus about a year of processing. Employment permit: 2031 now (about 6 years to a decision), 2034 under the Scheme (about 9). Spouse of an Irish citizen, married 3 years on arrival: 2029 (about 4) becomes 2031 (about 6). Master's graduate after a 1-year master's on Stamp 2: 2032 (about 7) becomes 2035 (about 10). PhD researcher after a 4-year doctorate on Stamp 2: 2035 (about 10) becomes 2038 (about 13). Temporary Protection: 2031 now; under the Scheme the clock does not run. Processing is ISD's statement that most applications are decided within 12 months.

## Post 2/7  [231 chars]

Spouses of Irish citizens: the marriage and the residence both go from three years to five.

Every applicant would also need a language test (Irish, English or Irish Sign Language) and a civics test, to standards the Minister sets.

## Post 3/7  [230 chars]  → image: metrics.png

Standard applicants face a new self-sufficiency test: a minimum income set by regulation, no prescribed welfare or housing support for two years (six months can be excused), and no debt to Revenue, Social Protection or the courts.

Alt: Three tiles titled The three self-sufficiency tests. Minimum income: not set, to be prescribed by Ministerial regulation. Welfare and housing: 2 years with no prescribed support, of which 6 months may be excused. Debt to the State: none allowed, covering Revenue, social welfare, or an unpaid court fine. Source: General Scheme Head 7 and new section 15(3) and (4).

## Post 4/7  [229 chars]

Some time would stop counting at all: Temporary Protection, time awaiting an EU treaty rights claim later refused, and residence obtained by fraud.

Student time on Stamp 2 already does not count. The Scheme leaves that as it is.

## Post 5/7  [202 chars]

Schedule 1 lists immigration offences that bar a grant outright, with no discretion.

Head 10 adds a new ground to revoke citizenship already granted: “public policy, public order or national security”.

## Post 6/7  [242 chars]  → image: passage.png

None of this is law yet. The General Scheme goes to the Joint Committee on Justice for pre-legislative scrutiny.

The income threshold, welfare lists and test standards come later, by regulation. Only Head 9 saves applications already lodged.

Alt: Five-stage track titled The Bill's own path, not the applicant's. Stage one, General Scheme, published September 2026, is marked reached. Still to come: pre-legislative scrutiny by an Oireachtas committee, the Bill drafted, Oireachtas passage through five Stages in each House, and commencement, phased and decided by the Minister. Below, a panel reads: The General Scheme is not law. The income threshold, welfare and housing lists, language and civics standards and waiver categories are set by regulation after enactment, not in the Act.
